Data process

How Kumquat Builds Data

Kumquat combines AI-generated crawlers, public web data, source monitoring, and structured cleanup to create practical lead lists for modern sales workflows.

1

Define the market

We identify a specific buyer segment, business category, or sales opportunity, such as restaurants without online ordering, contractors with weak web presence, venues without booking links, or local businesses missing key digital tools.

2

Generate targeted crawlers with AI agents

Kumquat uses AI agents and Codex-assisted workflows to generate focused crawler code for each data category. Instead of one broad collection workflow, we build task-specific collection around the exact market, fields, and signals needed.

3

Collect public business signals

Our crawlers gather publicly available business information from websites, public directories, business pages, search results, and other open web sources. This may include business names, websites, categories, locations, contact details, social links, and visible digital signals.

4

Monitor sources for updates

We use monitors to detect stale records, broken links, changed websites, missing pages, and new market opportunities. This helps improve data freshness and keeps lists from becoming static one-time scrapes.

5

Clean and structure the data

We remove obvious duplicates, normalize fields, standardize formatting, and prepare the data for CSV exports, CRM imports, enrichment workflows, AI sales agents, and outbound tools.

6

Add opportunity labels

Where possible, we tag why a lead may be useful, such as "no booking link," "no online ordering," "missing website," "weak local SEO," "no visible contact form," or "outdated web presence."

7

Deliver ready-to-use files

Buyers receive structured data they can inspect, enrich, upload, or pass directly into AI workflows. The goal is to provide a cleaner starting point, not a messy scrape.

Kumquat focuses on publicly available business information for B2B prospecting, market research, and AI sales workflows. Buyers are responsible for using data in compliance with applicable outreach and privacy rules.

Compliance

Built for responsible data use

Kumquat focuses on business-facing datasets, public-source research, and structured commercial intelligence. We do not position our products as consumer surveillance, sensitive personal data, or spam databases. Our goal is to help legitimate teams discover businesses, markets, creators, vendors, and opportunities using transparent, practical data.

California and privacy rules are evolving. California's Delete Act requires qualifying data brokers to register annually, and starting August 1, 2026, registered brokers must process deletion requests through the DROP system every 45 days. Even when a dataset is business-facing, Kumquat treats removal, correction, source transparency, and acceptable use as part of the product.
